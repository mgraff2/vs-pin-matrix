Pin Matrix is a spreadsheet for your waypoints. Every pin you own in one sortable, filterable table, edited a hundred at a time instead of one at a time on the map. Find the one you meant to keep, retint a whole class in one pass, switch off the clutter without deleting anything, and hand a location to a friend in a click.

Installs Client-side only. Works on any server, modded or not, and nobody else needs it.
Version Vintage Story 1.22.x, tested on every patch from 1.22.0 to 1.22.7.
Opening it P with the world map open, or the Pin Matrix Editor button. A rebindable hotkey ships unbound.

What you get

  • A real table. Every column sorts: name, icon, colour, X, Y, Z, distance, pinned, and whether the pin is on your map. Coordinates read the way your HUD reads them.
  • Six filters that compose. Search, icon, colour, pinned only, visible or hidden, and within N blocks. Stack them freely, and every count respects the ones above it.
  • Bulk everything. Select a range or the whole filtered set, then delete, recolour, re-icon, pin, unpin or rename the lot in one go.
  • Hide without deleting. Switch pins off the map and minimap while they stay on the server, in the table, ready to come back.
  • Pin sets. Save a filter as a one-click toggle in a drawer beside the world map.
  • Duplicate cleanup. Fold identical pins together, and untangle the three markers different tools left on the same trader.
  • Optional extras. Automatic trader markers, Herty cup markers, translocator paths you have actually walked, a snap grid for the map screen's windows, and an interface scale that follows the screen.

Nothing changes without a preview and a confirmation. Deletes go to a recycle bin, the last bulk operation is undoable, and only your own waypoints are ever touched.

The details

Click a heading to open it.

Finding a pin: filters, colours, and walking outward from where you stand

The colour filter shows the colours. Painted swatches with hex codes and live counts, sorted round the colour wheel with the greys first, listing only the colours your pins actually use. The counts follow your other filters, so you can see at a glance how many red pins are within 200 blocks.

The distance slider is an expanding search. Drag it out from off and your pins appear in growing rings around you, 10 blocks up to 10,000. Or press Next pin and it widens the radius by exactly enough to reveal the next-nearest pin, one click at a time, until it tells you there is nothing further out. Both respect every other filter, so "the next copper node I haven't visited" is one button.

Editing in bulk: select, preview, confirm, undo

Click rows, shift-click for a range, or take the lot with Select all filtered. Then delete, set colour, set icon, pin, unpin or rename every one of them at once.

Every bulk operation is previewed before it runs: a before and after list of exactly which pins change and how. Renames show the new titles, recolours show the old swatch beside the new one. Deleted pins go to a recycle bin you can restore from, Undo last bulk reverses the last operation whatever it was, and the config can write a full export before each one if you would like a belt as well as braces.

Single pins keep their own buttons. Edit opens the vanilla waypoint dialog, Move re-creates the pin at new coordinates, Map (or a double-click on the row) centres the world map on it, and New pin drops one where you stand.

Hiding and pin sets: switch off the clutter, one pin or a whole class

You only need to see every copper node when you are going after copper. Click the eye in a row's Vis column and that pin leaves the world map and the minimap, taking its marker, hover text and screen-edge arrow with it, while staying on the server, in the table, searchable and editable. In bulk it is the usual three steps: filter to an icon, Select all filtered, Hide. Instant both ways, with no confirmation screen and no chat spam. A standing N hidden count sits by the page controls so you can never forget you have pins parked.

Pin sets turn a filter into a map button. Filter the table to whatever you like, press Save as set..., give it a name, and it becomes a row in a drawer down the right-hand side of the world map. Click the row and every pin it matches leaves the map at once. Click again and they come back.

A set stores the question, not the answer. The filter runs again the moment you click it, so resin you marked this morning is covered by a set you made last week. The drawer starts shut, and its 16px pull re-colours while any set is hiding pins, because pins missing from your map with nothing on screen explaining why is the one thing this feature exists to prevent.

Duplicates and same-spot pins: fold them up, then clear them out

Flip Group duplicates and pins identical in every column collapse into a single row marked × N copies. Fix duplicates is labelled with the live count and removes the extra copies through the usual preview, always keeping one, and preferring to keep a copy that is still showing on your map. It scans your whole list rather than the filtered view, so a filter cannot hide copies from the cleanup.

Fix same-spot pins is the other half of the job: pins that share a place and nothing else. One trader ends up carrying a pin you placed by hand, a leftover from whatever auto-marker you used before, and Pin Matrix's own. It finds those sets and names the survivor of each rather than only the condemned, keeps Pin Matrix's own trader marker in preference, and never collapses two traders of different specialisations, so a camp survives intact. Grouping never chains either: everything in a set is close to the pin that opened it, not merely to its nearest neighbour, so a line of pins cannot swallow a village.

Automatic markers: optional, for traders you walk past, cups you tap and translocators you actually use

Traders. Off until you switch it on. Then walking past a trader drops a waypoint on it, coloured by what it deals in: nine specialisations, each colour editable with a live swatch and a per-row reset. The palette matches Waypointer's, so the same kind of trader reads as the same colour whichever mod put the marker there. Two distances are yours to set: how close you have to get before a trader is marked, and how near an existing pin counts as "already marked" (kept deliberately loose, because traders wander around their cart). Each mark is announced in chat in the same coordinates your HUD shows, and a trader is only marked once it has settled into place, so a chunk that has just loaded cannot leave a pin somewhere it is not.

Herty cups. With the Herty Cups mod installed and this switched on, putting up a cup or taking resin from one drops a waypoint titled after the tree it is tapping: "Herty cup: Pine". Only cups you have actually handled are marked, which is deliberate. The game never tells a client who placed a block, so a mod that scanned for cups would mark every tap on the server; marking what you touch gets yours by construction.

Translocators. Also off by default. Step through one and both pads get a waypoint, each named after the coordinates of the other end, with a line drawn between them on the world map. Nothing is recorded for a pad you merely walked past, which is the point of it. The hop you just took is drawn in a bright colour for twenty minutes before settling back to the normal one, so you can see which way you came. If another tool already left you a pile of markers, Adopt TL waypoints... converts them into Pin Matrix paths, previewed first, changing only titles, icons and colours.

All three produce ordinary waypoints. They sort, filter, recolour, hide, bulk-rename, bin and export exactly like a pin you placed by hand, and switching any of them off later leaves nothing behind that the matrix cannot manage. Because they live on the server, a translocator network you map on one machine is simply there when you log in from another. No save file, no sync folder, no import step.

Window layout and interface scale: optional, to tidy the map screen and keep it readable on any monitor

A snap grid for the map screen. Switch it on and a Layout Zones (Z) button joins the map: press it and the screen fills with a grid of your chosen size, anything from 1×1 to 50×50, numbered along the edges. Drag any window by its title bar, drop it on a zone, and it snaps there, and it comes back to the same place next time it opens.

Cells sitting under your coordinate readout, hotbar, chat or any mod's HUD are drawn red and will not take a drop, so a snapped window can never bury something you need to read. Untick Do not cover HUDs and the whole screen opens up. Nominate a row as a tab strip and everything dropped on it spreads evenly across the full width, re-spacing as things come and go. Zones are remembered as grid cells rather than pixel coordinates, so a new resolution or a different monitor re-derives your layout instead of scattering windows off-screen. Reset layout is a chat command as well as a button, because a button inside a window that ended up somewhere unclickable is no help at all.

Fit interface scale to the screen is the companion setting. Vintage Story never resizes a dialog, so on a screen half as wide a window takes twice the share of it, and the only lever is the GUI scale four clicks deep in Settings. Switch this on and Pin Matrix re-derives it whenever the screen changes size. It measures from a reference, the scale you last chose and the screen you chose it on, so going to a smaller screen and back returns you to exactly the scale you started from rather than somewhere near it. Set the scale yourself anywhere, including Vintage Story's own settings, and that becomes the new reference. It is off by default: a mod quietly resizing your entire interface had better be something you asked for.

Sharing, import and export: a pin in chat, or a file you can keep

Share sends a pin to chat or copies its command to the clipboard for Discord. Friends who also run Pin Matrix see a clickable add this waypoint link in chat, with the game's own confirmation prompt. Everyone else still gets the location as plain readable text, and the copied command works for anyone you paste it to.

Export everything, or just your selection, to a file, then import it on another world, another character or another computer. Re-imports skip anything you already have, so running one twice is safe.

Keys and commands

P / Z Open the editor, or show the layout zones, with the world map open
Double-click a row Centre the world map on that pin
.pinmatrix zones Show or hide the layout grid
.pinmatrix resetlayout
.pinmatrix unsnap [name]		 Put every window back, or release just one
.pinmatrix rescue Bring every off-screen window back into view, anyone's window, not just ours
.pinmatrix guiscale [n] Show or set the game's GUI scale, for when the screens are too big to read
.pinmatrix tlpaths List your translocator paths and where each label points
.pinmatrix dialogs
.pinmatrix whyblocked
.pinmatrix rescanhuds		 Troubleshooting: what the grid sees, why one cell refuses a drop, re-measure the HUDs

P and Z are not global binds. They exist only while the Pin Matrix buttons are on the map screen, so they cannot collide with anything in a long mod list, and they step aside the moment any text box has focus. Either can be switched off in the config, and the rebindable hotkey under Settings → Controls ships unbound, so nothing is taken from you on install.

What it will never do

  • Change anything without showing you first. Preview, confirmation, recycle bin, undo, and an optional automatic backup before each one.
  • Delete a waypoint to hide it. Hiding is a display filter and nothing more. Uninstall the mod and everything hidden comes straight back.
  • Touch waypoints that are not yours. Pins shared with you by group members are left out on purpose, because the game only allows managing your own.
  • Move one of your HUDs, or claim a global key. The layout grid measures HUDs and steers around them, and the shortcuts live on the map screen only.

Good to know

  • Because the mod is client-side, bulk operations go through the game's own chat commands, so the server echoes one "Ok, waypoint added" line per pin. Harmless, just chatty on a big job.
  • Which pins you have hidden is remembered per world on your own PC, at ModData/pinmatrix/hidden-<world>.json. Copy that across if you want it on a second computer. Window positions go in the game's own clientsettings.json, so a laptop and a desktop keep independent layouts.
  • Everything else lives in ModConfig/pinmatrix.json: rows per page, the bulk-operation throttle, recycle bin size, the auto-backup switch, the P and Z shortcuts, marker colours and distances, and the layout grid.
